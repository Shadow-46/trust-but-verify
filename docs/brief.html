Unique selling proposition

When a device is lost or a key must rotate, most systems keep trusting stale credentials and cannot prove what they decided. Our service revokes a device on every node in about 20 ms (3 local node processes), rotates keys with a documented cutoff while transfers finish, and protects files and messages with post-quantum cryptography against "harvest now, decrypt later". Every allow and every block is written with its reason into an ML-DSA-signed, hash-chained log and explained in plain language in the operator console, and the service fails closed on any integrity doubt — including an attacker rolling state back to undo a revocation. Zero dependencies: only vetted node:crypto (OpenSSL 3.5) primitives, no custom crypto or ML.

Architecture

  • Identity & authorization: every request re-checks owner, disabled and revoked lists; nodes also verify the device's ML-DSA signature, target node, nonce and key version.
  • Keys: master key (or a KMS via TRACK4_MASTER_KEY) → HKDF → key-encryption key and state-MAC key; versioned data keys. Rotation: 30 s grace, then every node refuses the old version; files re-encrypted in one atomic commit.
  • Logs & recovery: atomic writes, PID-checked locks, signed log head plus optional external witness, signed and digested backups whose restore keeps later revocations. Any doubt ⇒ integrity_failure.
  • Versions: Node 24.11.1 built-ins (ML-KEM-768, ML-DSA-65, X25519, AES-256-GCM, HKDF/HMAC-SHA-256); Next.js 16 for the console only.

Trust boundaries and adversaries

  • A1Unauthorized caller — malformed input, other users' IDs, injection, __proto__, path tricks.
  • A2Lost or stolen device — holds the device key and keeps trying after revocation.
  • A3Compromised valid account — privilege gain and lateral movement with valid credentials.
  • A4Network attacker — records, replays, re-addresses, drops, reorders, swaps handshake keys; may later hold a quantum computer or stolen identity keys.
  • A5Local attacker without keys — edits, rolls back or truncates state, logs, backups, policy; plants symlinks.
  • A6Compromised node — cannot forge or roll back policy for other nodes, but can wrongly allow its own requests until isolated.

Assumptions

Correct OpenSSL 3.5 primitives; trust anchor and identity keys provisioned out of band; key files private to the service user or a KMS-held master key; node clocks within ±30 s; setup is a trusted operator action.

Exclusions and remaining risks

  • Largest risk: full host compromise (keys + disk) allows decryption and a consistent rollback of state and log together; the rollback is detected only when the external witness (TRACK4_WITNESS_DIR) is configured, and key theft needs an external KMS.
  • No HSM; key erasure relies on Node process memory. Policy (HTTP) and messages (TCP) run on loopback without TLS; signatures and the hybrid handshake carry security.
  • Detector accuracy comes from public practice data and our synthetic generator, not production traffic. Not built, by choice: Merkle inclusion proofs, hybrid logical clocks, SLH-DSA, a DH ratchet (so no post-compromise security). Denial of service and side channels are out of scope.
